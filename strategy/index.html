Value proposition

For parents, primary schools and early years settings that want less waste without sacrificing quality: the same washable and safe Crayola markers, but refillable. You replace only the ink and the tip, so the marker lasts longer. Sold in a starter kit with refills per colour and, for schools, in a Classpack with a refill kit for the whole year, in the same shops and school suppliers as today.

Today: you buy, the kids use it, then you throw it away. With refill: you buy once, you refill, you change the tip and the kids reuse it. Concept

Segments · STP and jobs to be done

Today's customers and where we would aim

Today: parents, schools, nurseries and other buyers (children's art clubs, relatives buying gifts) who buy Crayola markers and replace them when they run out or dry out. There is no public evidence for the size of this group in the UK. Willingness / ability / motivation ratings below are Assumption.

Segment A · families

Parents of children aged 3–10 who care about the environment

  • Job: keep kids drawing, with markers that last and stay washable, without constantly throwing away and rebuying.
  • Willingness / ability / motivation: Medium / Medium / Medium.
  • Biggest barrier: refilling at home may feel like extra effort.
  • Trigger: back to school, or a marker running out. Assumption
  • Differs from today: partly — the same core need, plus caring about the environment and good habits.
  • Size: 8.5 million UK families with dependent children of all ages (ONS 2025). Upper bound: not all have children aged 3–10 or care about the environment.

Segment B · primary schools

Primary schools buying art supplies

  • Job: when restocking for the next school year, have markers that last all year, stay washable, stay on budget and meet environmental goals.
  • Willingness / ability / motivation: High / Medium / High.
  • Biggest barrier: teachers have little time, so refilling many markers may be too time-consuming.
  • Trigger: supplier orders for the next year or term. Assumption
  • Differs from today: partly — the same core need, plus reducing environmental impact and meeting sustainability goals.
  • Size: 16,712 state-funded primary schools and 4,470,363 pupils in England, 2025/26 [24] (England only; the UK figure is higher).

Segment C · early years settings

Nurseries, preschools and childminders (children 3–4)

  • Job: give children safe, washable markers that last, so daily activities run smoothly and parents see the setting cares about the environment.
  • Willingness / ability / motivation: Medium / High / Medium.
  • Biggest barrier: worry about leaks that children could reach.
  • Trigger: restocking for the next activities. Assumption
  • Differs from today: partly — the same core need, plus showing parents they care about the environment.
  • Size: 53,600 childcare providers with at least one child aged 0–4 (England only; includes ages 0–2, so not all fit 3–4).

Segment-rule check (measurable, large enough, stable, accessible, not overlapping, homogeneous): all three rated yes in the group's analysis. Sources: ONS Families and households; DfE Schools, pupils and their characteristics; DfE Childcare and early years provider survey 2025; GOV.UK sustainability leadership guidance for education; Oxford ECI researcher article on UK parents and going green. Porter's generic strategy: focused differentiation

Not aiming for the lowest price. The product differentiates through a refillable body and replaceable tip, and attracts an audience that cares about the environment.

VRIO

  • Refillable washable design: valuable and rare (we found no refillable washable kids' marker in the UK), but likely easy to imitate, as refill technology already exists in office markers. Temporary advantage.
  • Washable ink; presence in school supply channels; established children's brand: valuable, but BIC, STABILO and Berol have them too. Parity.
Evidence behind the canvas scores
  • Price: BIC £0.27, Super Tips £0.30 (six-retailer average), STABILO £1.57 per marker. Refill price is not set, so the 3 is an assumption.
  • Washable: Crayola, STABILO and BIC all claim washable ink (retailer and manufacturer pages). Ours is a goal, not yet tested.
  • Reuse, tip, end of life: none of the alternatives reviewed is refillable or has a replaceable tip; Crayola states its markers are made of mixed plastics and has closed its ColorCycle take-back programme.
  • School provisioning: Crayola Classpack of 144; BIC packs from 36 to 288; STABILO only packs of 8, 12 and 14.
  • Child suitability: Crayola from 3 years; BIC 5 and up; STABILO 3–99 with a triangular grip. Refilling is done by adults.
  • Special editions: Crayola sells pastel Super Tips in packs of 12, 20 and a 40 pack; BIC and STABILO classic colours only.

What could go wrong

Reuse schemes can fail when habits and convenience win — the course cases on reusable cups and "bags for life" are warnings. If refills feel fiddly, cost more per marker than a new one, or raise safety worries, adults will simply buy a new pack. Pending evidence from a survey or pilot.

Volume, profit and trade-offs

  • Volume: could fall if refills replace some pack sales (cannibalisation). Pending estimate.
  • Price: parts, handling and safety work may raise cost; whether to pass it on is undecided.
  • Profit: needs unit costs and margins. Pending no data yet.
  • Early adopters: likely to accept a higher price first (diffusion of innovations) — to be tested.
