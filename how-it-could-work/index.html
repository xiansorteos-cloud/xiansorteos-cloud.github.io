02 Designed to be refilled

See what's inside.

The ink soaks into a wick that stays inside the body. In this idea, a grown-up unscrews the back, clicks in a bottle of the same colour, and the wick takes up a measured dose — while kids keep drawing.

Still to test Leaks, how cleanly the bottle connects, the right dose, colour quality and washability. The refill picture is only illustrative.

03 A new tip, new possibilities

A new tip. More ways to draw.

When a tip wears out, the idea is to swap in a fresh one. Thin lines, bold strokes — the same marker, ready for the next picture.

Still to test Whether a swapped tip stays put, how it really draws, and whether fine and bold strokes are achievable.

Please read first

This is a design sequence, not a guide. It describes how a redesigned marker could be built to work. It does not apply to the Super Tips sold today, and nothing here explains how to open them. Please don't try to open, refill or re-tip markers you own: ink, small parts and closures have not been evaluated for that.

Concept design — refill mechanism not yet validated.

The proposed sequence in five steps

  1. Step 1 Proposed · adult

    Cap off, marker parked

    An adult takes the marker out of use and removes the cap. In the sketch the cap is simply set aside.

  2. Step 2 Proposed · adult

    Rear plug comes off

    The removable plug at the back is the proposed way into the body. How it would be held shut — and kept from loosening — is exactly what has not been designed or tested.

  3. Step 3 Proposed · adult

    A measured dose of the same colour

    The adult would click a bottle of the same colour into the back. The ink wick inside takes up a measured dose; nothing is taken out, and nobody pours ink by hand. Each bottle holds one colour and several doses. The bottle in the sketch is only an illustration: how it connects and how much ink a dose holds have not been defined.

  4. Step 4 Proposed · adult

    A worn tip, a fresh tip

    A spare tip would take the place of a worn one. Whether it stays put, feeds ink evenly and draws well is not known. In the sketch the faded tip is the worn one, set aside.

  5. Step 5 Proposed · adult

    Closed up, checked, back to drawing

    Everything goes back together and would need to be checked for leaks and secure closure before a child picks it up. What "checked" means is a safety-evaluation question.

Limits

What this proposal does not claim

  • It is not available, priced, or on sale.
  • No prototype exists. The drawings are provisional illustrations.
  • Threads, fittings and ribs in the drawings are only a visual language for "this part comes off".
  • It does not say refilling is safe for children to do. The proposal puts adults in charge, subject to evaluation.
  • It does not say it saves plastic, money, or time.

Still to prove

Tests that would have to happen

  • Leaks
  • Bottle connection and dose
  • Closure and loosening
  • Tip retention
  • Ink exposure
  • Small parts
  • Durability
  • Colour constancy
  • Fine and bold strokes
  • Washability on skin
  • Washability on most washable clothing
