Skip to the grown-ups' summary

For grown-ups

Ran out of blue? Refill the blue.

Today, when one marker dries out, it goes in the bin, and often a whole new pack comes home. Super Tips Refill is our idea for the same washable marker kids already know, built so a grown-up can replace just the ink and the tip, and keep the rest.

Today

You buy a pack, the kids use it, and when a colour runs out the whole marker is thrown away.

With Refill

You buy once. When a colour runs out, you refill that colour and change the tip if it's worn. The kids keep drawing with the same marker.

Still washable

The aim is the same washable ink families trust today. Any redesign would have to keep that before it reaches a single child.

Grown-ups refill. Kids draw.

Opening the marker, changing ink or swapping a tip is a job for an adult. We are not claiming a child could not open it: no standard defines that for a marker, and the closest test for the closure has not been done. See what would have to be tested → Please don't try to open the markers you own today. They are sealed and not built for it.

The five parts

  1. Cap

    Keeps the tip from drying out between drawings.

  2. Replaceable tip

    Swapped when it wears, so lines stay crisp.

  3. Reusable body

    The barrel kids hold. The part you keep.

  4. Ink wick

    Holds one colour and stays inside. When that colour runs out, a grown-up refills it from a bottle of the same colour.

  5. Rear plug

    A closed back, made for grown-up hands. Opening it is the adult's job, not the child's.

Please read first

This is a design sequence, not a guide. It describes how a redesigned marker could be built to work. It does not apply to the Super Tips sold today, and nothing here explains how to open them. Please don't try to open, refill or re-tip markers you own: ink, small parts and closures have not been evaluated for that.

The proposed sequence in five steps

  1. Step 1 Proposed · adult

    Cap off, marker parked

    An adult takes the marker out of use and removes the cap. In the sketch the cap is simply set aside.

  2. Step 2 Proposed · adult

    Rear plug comes off

    The removable plug at the back is the proposed way into the body. How it would be held shut — and kept from loosening — is exactly what has not been designed or tested.

  3. Step 3 Proposed · adult

    A measured dose of the same colour

    The adult would click a bottle of the same colour into the back. The ink wick inside takes up a measured dose; nothing is taken out, and nobody pours ink by hand. Each bottle holds one colour and several doses. The bottle in the sketch is only an illustration: how it connects and how much ink a dose holds have not been defined.

  4. Step 4 Proposed · adult

    A worn tip, a fresh tip

    A spare tip would take the place of a worn one. Whether it stays put, feeds ink evenly and draws well is not known. In the sketch the faded tip is the worn one, set aside.

  5. Step 5 Proposed · adult

    Closed up, checked, back to drawing

    Everything goes back together and would need to be checked for leaks and secure closure before a child picks it up. What "checked" means is a safety-evaluation question.

    For schools and nurseries

    Classpack + year refill kit

    A Classpack with a refill kit for the whole year. A school would add it to the order it already places with its usual stationery supplier, and could sign up for it to arrive each term instead of reordering. See schools & nurseries →

    You would find it where you buy Super Tips today: supermarkets, toy and craft shops, online, and school suppliers. This is a student concept: nothing here is on sale.

    Limits

    What this proposal does not claim

    • It is not available, priced, or on sale.
    • No prototype exists. The drawings are provisional illustrations.
    • Threads, fittings and ribs in the drawings are only a visual language for "this part comes off".
    • It does not say refilling is safe for children to do. The proposal puts adults in charge, subject to evaluation.
    • It does not say it saves plastic, money, or time.

    Still to prove

    Tests that would have to happen

    • Leaks
    • Bottle connection and dose
    • Closure and loosening
    • Tip retention
    • Ink exposure
    • Small parts
    • Durability
    • Colour constancy
    • Fine and bold strokes
    • Washability on skin
    • Washability on most washable clothing
