Draft positioning statement

For families and primary schools who want children to keep drawing without throwing away the whole marker, Super Tips Refill is a washable marker system with replaceable ink and tip that an adult can look after — unlike buying a new complete marker each time.

Points of parity: washable, colourful, child-friendly (to be shown). Points of difference: refillable reservoir, replaceable tip. Draft

Segments · STP and jobs to be done

Today's customers and where we would aim

Today (sourced): Crayola sells to families through packs of 12 and to schools through a 144-marker Classpack [2]. Segment sizes are Pending — the school and pupil figures from the first pitch are unverified.

Target segment A · hypothesis

Waste-aware families with young children

  • Profile: parents and carers of children roughly 3–8 who care about waste (demographic + psychographic).
  • Functional job: keep kids drawing without a constant stream of dried-out markers.
  • Emotional: feel good about not throwing things away.
  • Social: be seen as a thoughtful parent.
  • Differs from today: asks for a little adult effort and a different buying habit.

Pending size, willingness to pay, interviews.

Target segment B · hypothesis

Primary schools: teachers and the people who buy

  • Profile: classroom teachers (users) and school purchasing staff (buyers) — behavioural + geographic (UK).
  • Functional job: always have working markers without a big restock.
  • Emotional: less stress over supplies; confidence about child safety.
  • Social: show the school's values to parents and governors.
  • Differs from today: needs a refill routine, storage and an owner.

Pending number of schools (source), budgets, procurement rules.

What could go wrong

Reuse schemes can fail when habits and convenience win — the course cases on reusable cups and "bags for life" are warnings. If refills feel fiddly, cost more per marker than a new one, or raise safety worries, adults will simply buy a new pack. Pending evidence from a survey or pilot.

If one £1.50 refill topped up only one marker, it would cost many times a new £0.27 marker. Refills only compete on price if one refill serves several markers — the key definition we still need.

Volume, profit and trade-offs

  • Volume: could fall if refills replace some pack sales (cannibalisation). Pending estimate.
  • Price: parts, handling and safety work may raise cost; whether to pass it on is undecided.
  • Profit: needs unit costs and margins. Pending no data yet.
  • Early adopters: likely to accept a higher price first (diffusion of innovations) — to be tested.
