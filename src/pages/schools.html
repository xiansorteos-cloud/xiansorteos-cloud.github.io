Schools · hypotheses only

A classroom idea, kept honest.

Markers are everyday classroom tools. This page asks how a marker with replaceable ink and tips might fit into school life — and lists the practical questions to answer before anyone could say it does.

What is verified about today's school product

Crayola UK sells a Classpack of 144 Super Tips Washable Markers in 12 colours, described as washable, non-toxic and suitable from age 3 [2]. We have not verified school counts, pupil numbers, prices or market share, so none is used here.

Who's involved

People who might buy and use it

These roles are working assumptions. None has been interviewed or tested. To be validated

Pupils

They draw. In this concept they never open the marker, handle ink or touch small parts.

Teachers & classroom assistants

They hand markers out and tidy up. They'd want to know quickly what is a "kept" part and what is a "replaced" part.

School purchasing & business staff

They compare supplies and need clear, comparable information about parts, ordering and storage.

A named adult for refills

If the idea works at all, someone would need to be responsible for refilling and tip changes. Who that is would differ between schools.

Frictions

What could get in the way

Staff time

Refilling and swapping tips is extra work. Whether it is smaller or larger than today's routine for replacing markers is unknown.

To find out: how long it would take, and who has that time.

Storage

Spare ink and tips would need a safe, labelled place, out of children's reach.

To find out: what storage schools already have, and what the parts need.

Spills

Any time ink is handled, spills are possible. Washability would have to hold on skin, surfaces and most washable clothing.

To find out: how ink behaves when handled, and how to contain it.

Provisioning

Ordering the right ink and tip for the right marker, keeping stock, and replacing lost parts all need a system.

To find out: what ordering would need to look like.

What this page does not say

No school has tested this concept, so there are no results, usage numbers, savings or environmental figures here — because none exist. The concept is not available to buy and no prices are proposed.
