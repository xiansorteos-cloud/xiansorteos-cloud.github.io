For grown-ups

What changes, what is proposed, what is unproven

A short, plain summary for families, teachers and school buyers. Everything below describes a design idea, not a product you can buy or a mechanism that has been shown to work.

What changes

The idea is a physical redesign of a washable marker so it is built from separate parts: a cap, a replaceable tip, an ink reservoir, a reusable body and a removable rear plug.

The body stays the same piece kids hold. The ink and the tip become the parts that could be replaced.

What it might offer

These are hypotheses, not promises: a body that could stay in use after the ink or tip is spent, and a way to replace only what has worn out.

Whether this reduces plastic can only be judged by comparing whole life cycles, spare parts, packaging and transport. Being refillable does not prove it.

What nobody has shown yet

No prototype has been built or tested. Ease of opening, safety, leak-tightness, durability, drawing quality and washability are all unproven for this design.

Adults handle the refill — kids draw

In this proposal, opening the marker, swapping ink or changing a tip would be a job for an adult, and would only be offered after a safety evaluation (ink exposure, small parts, closure). It is not a how-to for the Super Tips sold today — please do not try to open markers you own.

The five parts

  1. Cap

    Covers the tip when the marker is not in use.

    Concept

  2. Replaceable tip

    A porous, tapered tip meant to be swapped when it wears. Retention and drawing quality are not yet tested.

    To be validated

  3. Reusable body

    The slim yellow barrel kids hold. The part the idea hopes to keep using.

    Concept

  4. Ink reservoir

    Holds the ink inside the body. Access, leaks and washability are not yet tested.

    To be validated

  5. Rear plug

    A removable closure at the back. Any thread or fitting shown is only visual language.

    To be validated
